# Build nutrition tracking around honest estimates

Specify a self-hostable, offline-capable nutrition module whose v1 combines manual entry, barcode lookup and an explicitly provisional energy estimate; do not position it as a medical or weight-loss treatment. Use open composition data and label-derived foods with provenance, but keep Open Food Facts (OFF) data operationally separate pending legal review. Treat missing nutrient values as unknown, not zero, and make logging neutral, editable and non-punitive. An adaptive TDEE can propose a weekly target change, but the user must accept it; it estimates maintenance in the user's own logging units, not laboratory-measured metabolism. Do not add wearable calorie expenditure to the same energy budget. Make safety controls and self-declared exclusions part of v1, while deferring opt-in AI photo and voice entry to editable drafts. The evidence supports cautious design, not numerical accuracy guarantees or a claim that tracking itself causes weight change.

## Seed estimates should yield to transparent trends

For an initial estimate, use a documented predictive equation with an uncertainty caveat rather than implying precision: the NASEM 2023 EER equations were derived from DLW data, but their reported prediction errors remain substantial; Mifflin–St Jeor is a defensible alternative for resting expenditure, with individual errors. ([NASEM 2023](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26818/chapter/7); [Frankenfield et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15883556/)) User-selected activity categories are themselves uncertain. Present the result as a starting estimate, not a measurement or clinical prescription.

After sufficient complete intake and weight history, a transparent energy-balance estimator can refine maintenance from logged intake and a smoothed weight trend. Weight change is not a fixed energy equivalent: glycogen and water affect early changes, and the energy content of weight loss changes over at least the first month. A static 3,500-kcal-per-pound rule is not a sound forward projection. ([Hall, “What is the required energy deficit per unit weight loss?”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17848938/); [Heymsfield et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3810417/); [Thomas et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23628852/)) The trend therefore needs uncertainty and a provisional state. The notes identify no peer-reviewed minimum-data threshold; do not turn proposed 14/28-day gates or missing-day thresholds into evidence-backed accuracy claims.

An adaptive estimate absorbs stable logging bias but cannot distinguish physiological expenditure from changing under-reporting, partial days, or other changes. Self-reported intake has been substantially below DLW in some studies, including a small, selected sample; this is not a universal correction factor. ([Lichtman et al. 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1454084/)) Require users to mark days complete, leave incomplete days missing rather than zero, and pause or qualify adaptation when completeness is inadequate. Weight is also affected by fluid shifts and other confounders; explain that the estimate is a trend, not a diagnosis of “slow metabolism.” Do not add wearable exercise calories on top of an activity-inclusive TDEE: that risks double-counting activity, while wearable expenditure accuracy was not established in the reviewed evidence.

## Separate food provenance from food identity

Use USDA FoodData Central (FDC) as the redistributable backbone for US generic foods and branded barcode products: USDA states its data are CC0/public domain, with attribution requested. ([FDC API guide](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide/); [FDC downloads](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/)) For generic regional foods, CoFID and Ciqual are promising attribution-licensed sources according to the notes, subject to checking dataset-specific terms and current versions. Do not bundle or redistribute Portugal's INSA/PortFIR table until written reuse permission is confirmed; free download is not an explicit reuse license. Commercial APIs with restrictive caching and redistribution terms do not fit a self-hostable core.

OFF is a useful global barcode source, particularly for European products, but its database is ODbL, its images are CC BY-SA, and its own terms disclaim completeness and accuracy. ([OFF terms](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/terms-of-use); [OFF data](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/data); [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/)) Keep an unmodified OFF mirror in a separate source-specific table, resolve it alongside—not into—a merged FDC/local-food database, and display linked attribution. This is a conservative architecture, **not a legal conclusion**: whether transformations, caching, or a hosted multi-tenant mirror trigger share-alike obligations needs counsel before release. Preserve source, source identifier, timestamp and known-field coverage for every item. Treat barcodes as lookup keys, not proof that a product or its label data are correct; variable-measure store codes need a private/manual path rather than global lookup.

## Logging must tolerate gaps without moral judgment

Manual logging, portion/quantity editing, grams and household measures, quick-add, recents, favourites, saved meals and meal copying make a practical core. A randomized pilot among 38 racial and ethnic minority adults found more diet-tracking days in its simplified arm than detailed arm (median 97% vs 49%); descriptive three-month weight changes were similar. The small pilot was not evidence that simplified tracking produces equivalent outcomes generally. ([Patel et al. 2022, DOI 10.2196/42191](https://doi.org/10.2196/42191); [PubMed 36512404](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36512404/)) This supports offering low-friction options, not a causal promise. Use a neutral diary: no “good/bad,” “cheat,” streak punishment, or red over-budget alarms. Offline mobile entry should write to a durable outbox and use cached foods; synchronize idempotently when connected, retaining provenance and surfacing conflicts rather than silently overwriting edits.

Keep nutritional fields nullable. A label or crowd entry with absent micronutrients cannot support a zero-intake inference; branded labels often omit nutrients, and app-database comparisons found weaker performance for sodium and cholesterol than for energy/macros. ([EU Regulation 1169/2011](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32011R1169); [Evenepoel et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7641788/)) Show coverage/completeness alongside totals, suppress “below target” signals when data are too incomplete, and never label a user deficient. Preserve the distinction between total, added and free sugars, and between salt and sodium; data sources do not consistently provide equivalent fields.

## Targets and safeguards are personal controls, not prescriptions

Offer maintenance and optional user-chosen change goals as wellness settings, with conservative constraints reviewed by a qualified clinician. The sources describe moderate deficits in clinical guidance, while very-low-calorie diets are supervised interventions; they do **not** establish a universal safe calorie floor for this product. ([NHLBI Practical Guide](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/files/docs/guidelines/prctgd_c.pdf); [NICE NG246, “Physical activity and diet”](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng246/chapter/physical-activity-and-diet)) Do not invent or hard-code a numerical minimum from the competing heuristics in the notes. Product must resolve the floor and goal-rate policy with clinical review before enabling generated deficit targets; until then, allow logging and maintenance/personal targets without automated restriction. Adaptive changes are weekly proposals requiring affirmative user acceptance, never automatic target changes.

Nutrient targets should identify jurisdiction, population, and reference type (for example, recommended intake versus adequate intake or upper limit), and should avoid diagnosis language. The notes verified WHO population guidance for selected limits and UK SACN's stricter free-sugars recommendation, but identified gaps in national reference values, licensing and local applicability. ([WHO healthy diet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet); [UK SACN](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/sacn-carbohydrates-and-health-report)) Do not ship an apparently comprehensive micronutrient target set until reference values, provenance, license and regional defaults are checked. Higher-protein suggestions are population-specific, not universal: a resistance-training meta-analysis reported a plateau in fat-free-mass gains around 1.62 g/kg/day, while the older-adult PROT-AGE position paper advises that people with severe kidney disease may need to limit protein. Neither is a universal target for this tracker. ([Morton et al. 2018, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608); [Bauer et al. 2013, DOI 10.1016/j.jamda.2013.05.021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23867520/)) Avoid medical claims or therapeutic targets.

Include a user-controlled hide-numbers mode and self-declared exclusions that disable automated deficit/protein recommendations while leaving neutral logging available. Evidence links tracker use with disordered-eating measures, but causal direction is unresolved and short-term experimental studies generally found no significant impact; safeguards are precautionary, not proof of harm or safety. ([Moody et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12547374/)) The exclusions, support resources and escalation copy require clinical/ED-specialist and jurisdictional review. Do not screen or infer diagnoses.

## Defer AI entry until drafts remain user-owned

Later photo and voice features should be opt-in, privacy-conscious and editable before saving. Photo-estimation studies show large meal- and setting-dependent errors; a free-living validation against DLW found substantial average underestimation and no within-person reliability in a small sample of 20 females with obesity. Voice evidence is pilot-scale, and nutrient accuracy from spoken descriptions remains largely unvalidated. ([Serra et al. 2026, “Limited validity of an AI-powered app for dietary assessment in females with obesity,” DOI 10.1038/s41746-026-02536-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-02536-2); [Chikwetu et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10230351/)) Show recognized foods, quantities and assumptions as a draft; require confirmation, retain edit provenance, and never imply clinical accuracy. Make third-party processing explicit and consent-gated; default to no location metadata and no unnecessary image retention. Do not describe this as an experimental MacroFactor clone: published vendor methodology is not peer-reviewed validation, its smoothing is undisclosed, and one vendor accuracy page conflicts internally on energy-density assumptions. ([MacroFactor methodology](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactors-algorithms-and-core-philosophy/); [accuracy page](https://macrofactor.com/algorithm-accuracy/)) An openhealth estimator needs its own transparent specification and validation before accuracy claims.

## Conclusion

The release boundary is clear: ship an honest manual/barcode diary with provenance, offline resilience, completeness-aware summaries, user-controlled targets and safety controls; keep adaptive targets explicitly provisional and user-accepted. Resolve the calorie floor and goal policy, target-value licensing and regional defaults, OFF compliance posture, and clinical review before enabling automated deficit generation or advertising validated accuracy. AI capture belongs later, as an optional assistant that drafts—not decides—what the user ate.
