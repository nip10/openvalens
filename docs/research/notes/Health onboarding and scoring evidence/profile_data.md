# Health-app profile fields and feature-specific evidence

## What data are scientifically necessary for which feature?

### Takeaway

There is no evidence basis for requiring a comprehensive demographic profile as a prerequisite for general health-app use. Inputs should be collected only when a specific feature actually needs them; energy equations are adult population prediction equations, not individualized measurements or a license to produce calorie prescriptions. Exact birth date is not needed for age-based calculations; collect a coarse age/eligibility band or age in years when the feature requires it. None of the cited sources supports universal pediatric, pregnancy, or sex-inclusive equation extrapolation.

### Inferences

- The lowest defensible profile is feature-dependent, not a fixed onboarding bundle: no age/sex/height/weight/activity required for ordinary general content; age eligibility only if there is an adult-only gate; height/weight/age and equation-relevant variable only when an adult energy estimate is explicitly requested.
- Exact DOB gives more precision than these features need and creates more sensitive identity data. This is data-minimization reasoning, not evidence of improved clinical efficacy. HIPAA minimum-necessary guidance is legally scoped and not a universal privacy law or scientific finding. [HHS, Minimum Necessary Requirement](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/minimum-necessary-requirement/index.html)
- “Unknown,” “prefer not to say,” and “not applicable” are distinct from “no.” Missing risk inputs should disable only the dependent personalization, not silently resolve to a negative answer.
- Self-reported anthropometrics should carry source/date and unit; measured and self-reported values should not be represented as equivalent certainty.

### Gaps

- No directly verified source in this research establishes a universal safe or accurate activity-factor method, weight-goal recommendation, or validated trans-inclusive/intersex-inclusive resting-energy equation. Do not invent one.
- No jurisdiction-neutral minor eligibility rule is established here. Product/legal/clinical owners must define geography, age threshold, consent, and whether minors are supported.
- Before collecting pregnancy, anticoagulant, smoking, or chronic-condition status for any scoring or recommendation, #97 needs jurisdiction-specific clinical-guideline and regulatory evidence; generic flags are not screening.
- Eating-disorder evidence identified here is largely associative; it does not quantify causality for this product or validate a particular mitigation. Weight-centric goals require dedicated safety and clinical review.
- The 1990 Mifflin paper is an adult derivation cohort and abstract-level source; it does not justify asserting demographic inputs alone yield individualized energy scores. Validation in intended users, including those outside the derivation population, remains necessary.
