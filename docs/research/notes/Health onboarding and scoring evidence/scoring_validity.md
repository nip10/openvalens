# Scientific validity of readiness and progress scoring

## Do current evidence support a daily readiness composite?

### Takeaway
No evidence reviewed supports committing to a general-adult, cross-device, cross-input numerical “readiness” composite at launch, much less asserting equivalence between manual and wearable users. Evidence supports transparently presenting component measurements, personal longitudinal trends, and self-reported state separately. Athlete HRV-guided training trials do not validate a universal consumer daily score.

### Inferences
- “Readiness today” and “long-term goal progress” are distinct constructs. Long-term progress may be presented as transparent, goal-specific descriptive quantities (for example, logged activity against a user-set target) without pretending heterogeneous domains share a validated scale.
- For today, a safer initial product is a component dashboard and plain-language explanation: what was measured, source, period, personal trend/context, and what is missing. A composite number would require prospective validation against a defined construct/outcome in intended general adult populations, calibration, subgroup/device analysis, and external validation.
- Personal baselines can improve interpretability but are not a magic validity guarantee. No universal minimum baseline duration, confidence cutoff, or cross-device conversion is established by the evidence cited here. Avoid asserting one.
- Acquisition protocol is essential: specify HRV metric (e.g., RMSSD vs vendor “HRV”), nocturnal vs waking, sensor/device/firmware, measurement window, activity/load definition, sleep algorithm, timestamps/timezone, and quality/missingness. Do not silently impute absent metrics or let device availability alter score meaning.
- Preserve raw inputs and score/model version if an experimental score is later tested; mark estimates, missing input, and version changes. Manual-only measurements are not interchangeable with wearable-derived values without direct comparison validation.

### Gaps
- No validated general-adult daily readiness instrument or universally validated wearable-plus-self-report composite identified in this lane. No evidence found for mapping commercial readiness scores to one another or to PAI.
- Validated brief daily instruments covering all requested domains (energy, stress, soreness, fatigue) with broad general-adult daily-readiness validity and confirmed commercial licensing were not established. PROMIS fatigue is construct/recall-specific; exact permission for each proposed version and mode requires instrument-owner confirmation.
- Published device validation is model-, algorithm-, metric-, and cohort-specific; evidence cannot establish validity for every device or manual entry. Future versions need explicit target population and validation plan before numerical composite commitment.

## What wearable, load, and questionnaire inputs can claim about readiness?

### Takeaway
Signals support careful within-person description and, in selected athlete contexts, training-response monitoring—not causal claims, injury prediction, or generalized “how ready are you?” assertions. Clinical/general adult users must not inherit athlete-only evidence claims.

### Cited Findings
- HRV-guided training meta-analysis involved only 198 endurance-training participants across eight studies; performance/VO₂peak pooled effects were not significant. [Düking et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34489178/)
- Consumer sleep tracker PSG comparisons establish measurement agreement only for tested devices/cohorts and sleep metrics, not a valid sleep-quality or readiness score. [11-device PSG validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10654909/)
- Athlete self-report systematic review concerns responsiveness to training in athletes; that does not establish clinical interpretation or general-adult norms. [Saw et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2015-094758)
- Activity tracker intervention effects on activity do not show that any vendor or app progress index measures health or goal attainment validly. [Ferguson et al. 2022](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landig/article/PIIS2589-75002200111-X/fulltext)

### Inferences
- Explanations should use bounded language (“your logged sleep estimate was shorter than your recent recorded pattern”) rather than normative judgments (“poor recovery,” “not ready”) unless the latter is independently validated for the target population and use.
- Self-report can capture perceived energy/stress/soreness, which sensors do not directly measure; preserve as a separate subjective domain and avoid treating disagreement as error.
- Onboarding should communicate that missing or incompatible data yields no comparable composite rather than a misleading default. No science-backed minimum-days rule is asserted here.

### Gaps
- This research lane did not validate all particular product inputs for proposed issues #95/#92; exact scope and planned formulas must be checked by parent owner against these constraints.
- No causal evidence identified that a composite daily score improves outcomes for general adults, nor evidence supporting equivalence of a manual and wearable-user score.
