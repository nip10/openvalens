# Preventive-care guideline landscape for age/sex-based reminders (US, UK, EU/Portugal)

Retrieval date for all sources: 2026-10-02 (unless noted). Scope: asymptomatic, non-pregnant adults 18+. Longevity/supplement claims and the app's own regulatory status are out of scope.

Note on source quality: rows marked "(search snippet)" were taken from search-engine summaries and **not** confirmed by fetching the primary page. Check them before encoding.

## 1. USPSTF: current A/B (and key C/I) recommendations for adults, and the 2025–2026 governance turmoil

### Takeaway

On paper the USPSTF A/B list for adults has not changed since mid-2025. It covers about 30 adult-relevant services, including breast biennial 40–74 (B, 2024), colorectal 45–75, cervical 21–65 (still the 2018 version), lung 50–80 with 20 pack-years, AAA, hypertension, statins, prediabetes 35–70, depression/anxiety, osteoporosis, HCV, HIV and others. In practice the Task Force barely functioned from mid-2025 to September 2026: meetings were cancelled, vice-chairs were fired, AHRQ staff was cut, and eight largely specialist members were appointed in September 2026. The current list is a frozen snapshot that may soon be revised in unpredictable ways.

### Cited Findings

**Current A/B recommendations relevant to non-pregnant adults** (from the USPSTF A&B list, fetched 2026-10-02) — [USPSTF A and B Recommendations](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation-topics/uspstf-a-and-b-recommendations)

- Abdominal aortic aneurysm screening: men 65–75 who have ever smoked. Grade B, Dec 2019.
- Anxiety disorders screening: adults 64 or younger. Grade B, Jun 2023.
- Breast cancer screening: women 40–74, biennial mammography. Grade B, Apr 30 2024. — also [USPSTF Breast Cancer Screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/breast-cancer-screening)
- BRCA risk assessment: women with a personal or family history of breast, ovarian, tubal or peritoneal cancer, or with ancestry associated with BRCA1/2. Grade B, Aug 2019.
- Cervical cancer screening: women 21–65. Grade A, Aug 2018 (details below).
- Chlamydia screening: sexually active women 24 or younger, and women 25+ at increased risk. Grade B, Sep 2021.
- Colorectal cancer screening: adults 45–49 (Grade B) and 50–75 (Grade A), May 2021.
- Depression screening: adults. Grade B, Jun 2023.
- Falls prevention (exercise): community-dwelling adults 65+. Grade B, Jun 2024.
- Healthy diet and physical activity counseling: adults with CVD risk factors. Grade B, Nov 2020.
- Hepatitis B screening: adolescents and adults at increased risk. Grade B, Dec 2020.
- Hepatitis C screening: adults 18–79. Grade B, Mar 2020.
- HIV screening: ages 15–65. Grade A, Jun 2019.
- HIV PrEP: people at increased risk. Grade A, Aug 2023.
- Hypertension screening: adults 18+. Grade A, Apr 2021.
- Intimate partner violence screening: women of reproductive age. Grade B, Jun 2025.
- Latent TB screening: asymptomatic adults at increased risk. Grade B, May 2023.
- Lung cancer screening: adults 50–80 with a 20 pack-year history (who currently smoke or quit within the past 15 years, per the 2021 statement). Grade B, Mar 2021.
- Obesity: behavioral weight-loss interventions for BMI ≥30. Grade B, Sep 2018.
- Osteoporosis screening: women 65+, and postmenopausal women under 65 at increased risk. Grade B, Jan 2025.
- Prediabetes / type 2 diabetes screening: asymptomatic adults 35–70 with overweight or obesity. Grade B, Aug 2021.
- Skin cancer counseling: up to age 24 only. Grade B, Mar 2018.
- STI behavioral counseling: people at increased risk. Grade B, Aug 2020.
- Statin use for primary CVD prevention: adults 40–75 with risk factors (and ≥10% 10-year risk per the full statement). Grade B, Aug 2022.
- Syphilis screening: people at increased risk. Grade A, Sep 2022.
- Tobacco cessation interventions: adults. Grade A, Jan 2021.
- Unhealthy alcohol use screening: adults 18+. Grade B, Nov 2018.
- Unhealthy drug use screening: adults 18+. Grade B, Jun 2020.

- Impaired visual acuity in asymptomatic adults 65+: Grade I, May 24 2022. — [USPSTF Visual Acuity](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/impaired-visual-acuity-screening-older-adults)

**Governance turmoil**

- _Kennedy v. Braidwood Management_ (Supreme Court, 2025) held that the Task Force falls under the HHS Secretary's authority, which widened his power to remove members. — [STAT, 2026-09-17](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/17/kennedy-names-eight-new-members-uspstf-task-force/)
- In July 2025 Kennedy signalled he would remove members he considered too "woke", and Task Force meetings were cancelled. — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/17/kennedy-names-eight-new-members-uspstf-task-force/); [NPR, 2025-07-27](https://www.npr.org/2025/07/27/nx-s1-5481828/rfk-jr-uspstf-advisory-panel-primary-care); [Columbia Sabin Center](https://climate.law.columbia.edu/content/rfk-jr-cancels-us-preventive-services-task-force-meeting)
- In May 2026 vice-chairs John Wong and Esa Davis were dismissed. — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/17/kennedy-names-eight-new-members-uspstf-task-force/); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/regulatory/kennedy-dismisses-leaders-us-preventive-services-task-force)
- STAT reported the panel was "largely defunct" for more than a year, with the website's most recent update dated August 2025. — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/17/kennedy-names-eight-new-members-uspstf-task-force/)
- AHRQ, which supports the Task Force, went from about 300 staff in 2024 to 84 in 2026. — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/17/kennedy-names-eight-new-members-uspstf-task-force/)
- On Sept 17 2026, eight new members were appointed:
  - The new chair is Seth Corey, a pediatric hematologist-oncologist.
  - Five of the eight are specialists (e.g. GI, oncology, cardiology, radiology), unlike the traditional primary-care/generalist make-up.
  - No meeting date had been set.
  - Sources: [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/17/kennedy-names-eight-new-members-uspstf-task-force/); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/regulatory/kennedy-appoints-8-new-us-preventive-services-task-force-members); [Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/uspstf-eight-new-members-kennedy-no-meeting-date-478858)
- Before the pause, pending topics included cervical cancer, breast cancer genetic screening and pediatric autism screening. Their status is unclear. — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/17/kennedy-names-eight-new-members-uspstf-task-force/)

### Inferences

- The A/B set above is a sound snapshot of the pre-2025 evidence-based consensus. Rules should be pinned to a specific statement and its release date, not to "whatever USPSTF currently says". Any 2026–2027 output from the reconstituted panel should be reviewed by a human before it is adopted.
- Many A/B items depend on risk factors (smoking history, BMI, CVD risk, STI/HIV risk, TB risk). A purely age/sex engine can only cover a subset safely:
  - Fully age/sex-driven: colorectal, breast, cervical, BP, HCV, HIV (15–65), depression, anxiety (≤64), alcohol, osteoporosis (65+), falls (65+).
  - Need an extra input: lung (pack-years), AAA (ever smoked), prediabetes (BMI), statins (CVD risk).
- The C (PSA) and I (vision, hearing) statements should not become "due" reminders. At most they should be "discuss with clinician" informational items.

- The 2018 PSA statement page was not fetched directly; the grades come from secondary snippets.
- I did not fetch the full statin statement (≥10% risk threshold) or the lung "quit within 15 years" wording. Both should be verified on the recommendation pages.
- I could not confirm whether any recommendation was formally withdrawn or changed after September 2026.

## 2. UK: UK NSC / NHS screening programmes and NHS Health Check

### Takeaway

England runs organised, invitation-based programmes, which differ from the US in several ways:

- Cervical: 25–64, every 5 years with HPV-primary testing.
- Breast: 50–70 (the US starts at 40).
- Bowel: 50–74 every 2 years by FIT (the US starts at 45).
- Lung: 55–74 ever-smokers, via targeted lung health checks.
- AAA: one scan for men at 65.
- NHS Health Check: every 5 years at 40–74 for people without existing CVD, diabetes or related conditions.

### Cited Findings

**NHS programmes in England** (nhs.uk screening hub, last reviewed 30 Jul 2024) — [NHS screening](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/nhs-screening/)

- Cervical: "women and people with a cervix aged 25 to 64", "every 5 years".
- Breast: "women aged 50 to 70"; self-referral is possible over 70.
- Bowel: "Everyone aged 50 to 74", "every 2 years"; self-referral at 75+.
- Lung: "aged 55 to 74 and have ever smoked".
- AAA: "men when they turn 65"; self-referral is possible over 65.
- Diabetic eye: everyone with diabetes from age 12.

**NHS Health Check**

- Ages 40–74 without pre-existing heart disease, diabetes, stroke, high blood pressure, etc. Invited "every 5 years".
- Covers BMI and waist, blood pressure, cholesterol, family history, smoking, alcohol and activity.
- Page last reviewed 14 Aug 2023; its scheduled next review (14 Aug 2026) has passed. — [NHS Health Check](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/nhs-health-check/)

### Inferences

- The main US–UK differences:
  - Breast start age: 40 (US) vs 50 (UK).
  - Bowel start age: 45 vs 50.
  - Cervical start age: 21 vs 25, and the UK screens every 5 years with HPV-primary only.
  - Lung: 50–80 with pack-years (US) vs 55–74 ever-smoked (UK).
  - AAA: a 65–75 window for ever-smokers (US) vs a single invite to all men at 65 (UK).
  - UK has no statin or HCV population screening as a reminder; the NHS Health Check bundles CVD risk assessment instead.
- UK programmes are call/recall, so the NHS sends the invitation. An app reminder is better framed as "expect/attend your invitation", not "book a test".
- England's rules may differ from Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Treat them as per-nation rules.

### Gaps

- I did not fetch UK NSC (gov.uk) pages for formal recommendation status, or Scotland/Wales/NI variations.
- Lung programme national rollout status and screening interval, and the bowel FIT threshold, were not verified.
- The NHS Health Check page is past its review date; current content should be re-checked.

## 3. EU: Council Recommendation on cancer screening (2022) and Portugal (DGS/SNS)

### Takeaway

The Council Recommendation of 9 Dec 2022 is non-binding. It recommends:

- Breast: mammography for women 50–69, with optional extension to 45–74.
- Cervical: HPV testing at 30–65 every 5+ years.
- Colorectal: FIT triage at 50–74.
- Lung, prostate and gastric: stepwise evaluation/piloting, not population screening.

Portugal's SNS programmes match the extended ranges:

- Breast: 45–74, biennial.
- Cervical: 30–69, 5-yearly HPV, extended by DGS Norma 09/2024 with self-sampling.
- Colorectal: 50–74.

### Cited Findings

**EU Council Recommendation (9 Dec 2022)**

- It recommends breast screening for women 50–69 by mammography and suggests extending to 45–74. — [Council press release](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2022/12/09/council-updates-its-recommendation-to-screen-for-cancer/); [EUR-Lex summary](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/promoting-cancer-screening-in-the-european-union.html) (search snippet)
- Cervical: HPV testing for women 30–65, "every 5 years or more", taking HPV vaccination status into account. — same sources (search snippet)
- Colorectal: FIT triage testing at 50–74 before endoscopy/colonoscopy follow-up. — same sources (search snippet)
- Target: 90% of the eligible EU population offered breast, cervical and colorectal screening by 2025. — same sources (search snippet)
- Lung, prostate and gastric are proposed for stepwise implementation/evaluation, not routine screening. — [Commission proposal COM(2022) 474](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/TXT/?uri=celex:52022DC0474); [EP Legislative Train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/carriage/cancer-screening/report?sid=7101) (search snippet; the full text could not be fetched because EUR-Lex returned empty content)

**Portugal**

- Breast: 45–74, mammography every 2 years.
- Cervical: people with a cervix aged 30–69, every 5 years.
- Colorectal: 50–74, faecal occult blood test.
- Source: [SNS24 – Rastreios oncológicos](https://www.sns24.gov.pt/pt/tema/prevencao-e-cuidados-de-saude/rastreios-oncologicos/) (search snippet)
- DGS Norma 09/2024 (17 Oct 2024) is the population-based cervical screening programme. Press coverage says it extended the age range to 69 and introduced self-collection. — [DGS Norma 09/2024](https://www.dgs.pt/normas-orientacoes-e-informacoes/normas-e-circulares-normativas/norma-092024-de-17102024-programa-de-rastreio-de-base-populacional-do-cancro-do-colo-do-utero1.aspx); [Público, 2024-10-18](https://www.publico.pt/2024/10/18/sociedade/noticia/rastreio-cancro-colo-utero-vai-alargado-ate-69-anos-preve-autocolheita-2108564)
- The legal basis for population-based screening programmes is Despacho n.º 8254/2017. — [Diário da República](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/despacho/8254-2017-108189401)
- DGS reports that breast screening exceeded its 2025 target. — [DGS](https://www.dgs.pt/em-destaque/rastreio-do-cancro-da-mama-portugal-ultrapassa-meta-prevista-para-2025-pdf.aspx) (title only, not fetched)

### Inferences

- The EU text is a framework, and member-state programmes decide the actual ages and intervals. The app should encode national programmes (e.g. PT) and use the EU Recommendation only as a default or fallback.
- Portugal is broader than the UK on breast (45–74 vs 50–70) but starts cervical later than the US (30 vs 21). Colorectal starts at 50, versus 45 in the US.

- I did not verify the Portuguese colorectal test interval (expected 2 years) or the exact Norma numbers for breast and colorectal.
- Other EU states were not covered.

## 4. Adult vaccination schedules (CDC/ACIP, UK JCVI/NHS, Portugal PNV/ECDC)

### Takeaway

Adult vaccination is highly jurisdiction-dependent. Ages, products and eligibility all differ:

- Shingles: 50+ (US) vs phased 65/70+ (UK).
- RSV: 75–79 (UK).
- Pneumococcal: 50+ (US) vs 65 (UK).
- Td boosters: at fixed ages (PT) vs no routine adult booster.

The US schedule itself is unstable because of 2025–2026 ACIP changes and litigation.

### Cited Findings

- **US (ACIP), 2025–2026 changes:**
  - COVID-19 moved from a universal recommendation to shared clinical decision-making.
  - Annual flu vaccination for everyone 6 months+ was reaffirmed for 2025–26, and thimerosal-containing flu vaccines were discontinued.
  - Pneumococcal vaccination is now recommended for adults 50+, and RZV (shingles) for 50+.
  - A federal court in March 2026 reportedly ruled that changes since Kennedy took office did not follow established procedures, and stayed them.
  - (search snippets; not confirmed against primary documents) — [CRS IN12684](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12684) (fetch returned 403); [AAFP 2026 schedules](https://www.aafp.org/about/blog/2026-immunization-schedules); [CDC adult schedule PDF](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/downloads/adult/adult-combined-schedule.pdf)
- **UK (JCVI/NHS):**
  - Shingrix (2 doses), phased in from Sept 2023, starting with people turning 65 and 70.
  - Pneumococcal: a single dose at 65.
  - RSV: ages 75–79 from 1 Sept 2024.
  - Source: [NHS England SW Adult Vaccination Toolkit 2025/26](https://www.england.nhs.uk/south/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2025/08/Adult-Vaccination-Programmes-Toolkit-SW-2025-26-v5.0.pdf) (search snippet)
- **Portugal (PNV):**
  - Since PNV 2017, Td boosters are given at 10, 25, 45 and 65, then every 10 years.
  - Source: [DGS – Novo PNV](https://www.dgs.pt/em-destaque/novo-programa-nacional-de-vacinacao2.aspx); [DGS Norma 018/2020](https://www.dgs.pt/normas-orientacoes-e-informacoes/normas-e-circulares-normativas/norma-n-0182020-de-27092020-pdf.aspx) (search snippet)

### Inferences

- Vaccines should be a separate, per-jurisdiction rule pack with its own owner and review cadence. Because the US schedule is disputed, a US pack may need to cite both CDC and professional-society schedules (AAFP/ACP/AAP) and flag the divergence.
- Seasonal items (flu, COVID) need annual date-based rules, not age-interval rules.

## 5. Dental, vision and hearing check intervals

### Takeaway

No strong guideline supports fixed universal intervals:

- Dental: NICE CG19 says dental recall should be risk-based, 3–24 months for adults (not "every 6 months").
- Hearing (50+) and vision (65+): USPSTF gives I statements (insufficient evidence).

### Cited Findings

- NICE CG19 dental recall:
  - Intervals are set by clinical assessment.
  - Shortest interval: 3 months. Longest for adults 18+: 24 months.
  - Over 24 months is discouraged.
  - Source: [NICE CG19 (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/n/nicecg19guid/pdf/); [NICE CG19 review decision 2012](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg19/documents/cg19-dental-recall-review-decision2)
- USPSTF hearing loss screening in adults 50+: I statement (2021). — [USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/announcements/final-recommendation-statement-screening-hearing-loss-older-adults)
- USPSTF impaired visual acuity screening in adults 65+: I statement (2022). — [USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/impaired-visual-acuity-screening-older-adults)

### Inferences

- Dental: use a user-configurable interval, defaulting to the dentist-assigned interval, bounded 3–24 months, and cite CG19. Do not hard-code 6 months.
- Vision and hearing: optional, low-importance "consider" reminders labelled as not evidence-graded. Diabetic eye screening is the exception and is condition-triggered.

### Gaps

- I did not check whether CG19 has been updated since its 2004 publication and 2012 review decision.
- Professional-body intervals (e.g. AAO eye exams) were not reviewed.

## 6. Grading systems and mapping them to app "importance" bands

### Takeaway

USPSTF letter grades map naturally onto bands. NHS/EU/PT programmes are organised programmes, not graded statements, so they need a separate "national programme" label instead of a forced letter-grade equivalent.

### Cited Findings

- USPSTF describes A/B as having "high or moderate net benefit", the services it "most highly recommends implementing". — [USPSTF A&B](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation-topics/uspstf-a-and-b-recommendations)
- Uses of the other grades in this note:
  - C: PSA at 55–69, framed as an individual decision.
  - D: e.g. cervical screening under 21 or over 65 with adequate prior screening.
  - I: vision and hearing screening.
  - Sources: [Cervical](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/cervical-cancer-screening); [Vision](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/impaired-visual-acuity-screening-older-adults)

### Inferences

Suggested honest mapping:

- **A/B** → "Recommended".
- **National organised programme** (NHS/SNS/EU) → "Offered by your health system".
- **C** → "Discuss with clinician".
- **I** → "Evidence uncertain" (informational only).
- **D** → suppress, or show "not recommended".
- **NICE/consensus intervals** (e.g. dental) → "Practice guidance".

Do not merge GRADE certainty ratings into these bands, because GRADE rates certainty of evidence and strength of a recommendation, which are not the same as USPSTF net-benefit grades.

### Gaps

- I did not fetch the official USPSTF grade-definition page, GRADE (strong/conditional) definitions, or NICE's "offer/consider" wording conventions. Cite those primary pages before finalising band labels.

## 7. Maintenance cadence, machine-readable feeds and licensing

### Takeaway

Change cadence:

- USPSTF topics are revised on multi-year cycles; the dates above span 2018–2025, roughly every 5–8 years per topic. Its output is now paused or unpredictable.
- NHS/DGS programmes change by policy decision (e.g. bowel to 50, PT cervical to 69 in 2024).

Feeds:

- USPSTF offers a REST/JSON "Prevention TaskForce" API filtered by age, sex and risk factors.

Licensing:

- USPSTF allows redistribution only verbatim, not for a fee or in a profit-making venture, with the USPSTF page cited and an AHRQ non-endorsement disclaimer.
- NICE's UK Open Content Licence is UK-only. It forbids altering recommendation wording and explicitly excludes AI use.

The safest pattern is to encode facts (ages, intervals, grades) with links, rather than copying guideline text.

### Cited Findings

**USPSTF Prevention TaskForce API**

- A REST API that returns USPSTF recommendation data as JSON, queried by patient characteristics (age, sex, behavioural risk factors), for integration into third-party apps. — [USPSTF API](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/apps/api.jsp); [API instructions PDF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/files/preventiontaskforce_data_api_wi.pdf)

**USPSTF copyright notice**

- AHRQ allows reproduction and redistribution "without any changes to the work… except as permitted as fair use".
- The work may not be "reproduced, reprinted, or redistributed for a fee, nor… sold for profit or incorporated into a profit-making venture".
- The USPSTF web page must be cited as the source.
- Adaptations need a disclaimer that AHRQ cannot endorse them.
- Implied endorsement of commercial products is prohibited.
- Source: [USPSTF Copyright Notice](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation-topics/copyright-notice)
- A search snippet claimed "USPSTF publications are in the public domain". This conflicts with the restrictive copyright notice above, so treat it as unverified.

**NICE UK Open Content Licence**

- It is UK-only, royalty-free, and allows copying, distribution, translation and commercial use.
- It does not allow amending or adapting the wording or structure of recommendations, or display next to advertising.
- CKS and BNF are excluded.
- Use of NICE content for AI purposes is not covered and needs separate permission.
- Use outside the UK needs a separate licence.
- Sources: [NICE reusing our content](https://www.nice.org.uk/reusing-our-content); [NICE UK Open Content Licence](https://www.nice.org.uk/reusing-our-content/nice-uk-open-content-licence)

**Recent changes**

- UK lung screening at 55–74 is now listed among national programmes. — [NHS screening](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/nhs-screening/)
- Portugal changed cervical screening in Oct 2024. — [DGS Norma 09/2024](https://www.dgs.pt/normas-orientacoes-e-informacoes/normas-e-circulares-normativas/norma-092024-de-17102024-programa-de-rastreio-de-base-populacional-do-cancro-do-colo-do-utero1.aspx)
- The US changed breast screening (2024), osteoporosis (2025), and the ACIP adult schedule (2025–26). See the sources above.

### Inferences

- Rule pack design:
  - Each rule stores: jurisdiction, body, recommendation ID/URL, grade or programme status, release date, retrieval date and a "last reviewed by maintainer" date.
  - Short paraphrased eligibility facts with a deep link are very likely lower-risk than embedding statement text.
  - **Not legal advice.** Facts like age ranges are generally not copyrightable, but this needs legal confirmation.
- If an AI feature summarises NICE text, that likely falls outside the NICE open licence.
- The USPSTF "no profit-making venture" clause matters if a hosted or commercial OpenValens offering ever ships verbatim text.
- Do not rely on the USPSTF API alone:
  - Its availability during the AHRQ staff cuts is unknown.
  - It is US-only.
  - A pinned, versioned, human-reviewed local rule set, with an optional diff check against the API, is more robust.
- Suggested review cadence: quarterly scan of USPSTF, NHS screening, DGS normas and CDC schedules, plus an annual seasonal vaccine update.

### Gaps

- I did not verify whether the USPSTF API is currently operational, needs a key, or has its own terms of use.
- I found no NHS screening-eligibility API. The NHS Website Content API (with its own licence) was not researched.
- No machine-readable feed was found for UK NSC, EU or DGS. All appear to be HTML/PDF only (unverified).
- DGS/SNS and EU content licensing terms were not researched.
