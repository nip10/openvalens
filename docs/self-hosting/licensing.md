# Licensing and trademark

**Applies to:** OpenValens 0.1.0 · Last updated: 2026-10-14

A summary for people who run OpenValens. The licence texts and the trademark
policy are what count; this page does not replace them and is not legal
advice.

## Licence

OpenValens is free software under the
[GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 or later](../../LICENSE)
(AGPL-3.0-or-later), with an
[additional permission for app-store distribution](../../LICENSE-APP-STORE).
Copies of both are also in [`LICENSES/`](../../LICENSES), and
[`REUSE.toml`](../../REUSE.toml) applies them to every file in the repository.

What that means for a self-hoster:

- **Running it unmodified** needs nothing beyond keeping the licence notices.
  (If you offer it to others as a service, also read the trademark section.)
- **Running a modified version that other people use over the network**
  triggers AGPL section 13: you must offer those users the corresponding
  source of your modified version, for example with a link to a public
  repository of your fork.
- **Distributing** the software or a modified version (including container
  images you publish) requires providing the source under the same licence.
- **App-store builds** of the mobile app may be distributed through stores
  whose terms would otherwise conflict with the AGPL, provided the exact source
  is available for free elsewhere and the app or its listing says where
  ([LICENSE-APP-STORE](../../LICENSE-APP-STORE)).

Contributions are accepted under the same terms with a DCO sign-off
([CONTRIBUTING.md](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#licensing-and-sign-off-dco)).

## Trademark

The licence covers the code, not the name. The
[trademark policy](../../TRADEMARK.md) sets out how the **OpenValens** name and
logo may be used. In short, for operators:

- Unmodified official releases may be redistributed under the name, and a
  self-hosted instance may show a "Powered by OpenValens" link.
- A **modified build or fork**, an app-store build not made from an official
  release tag, and **any hosted, multi-tenant or paid service you offer to
  others** must use a different name. You may say it is "based on
  OpenValens".
- Do not use the marks in app names, package identifiers or domain names, and
  do not imply the project endorses you or attribute medical claims to it.

Ask on the repository (a discussion or issue) when in doubt.
